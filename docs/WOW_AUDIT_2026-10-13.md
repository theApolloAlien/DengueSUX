## 2. The five things that would change the result

1. **One unbroken shot from the globe to a corridor.** Globe → Singapore island rising → click an area → prisms sink, HDB blocks rise in a stagger → camera descends to the route start → the walk begins → at the stop, the camera drops to eye level and the corridor band glows. Today each of those is a separate scene with cuts.
2. **A sun.** Light anchored to the map with a real Singapore sun position, a dawn → dusk slider, projected ground shadows from the estimated heights, and the existing "Aedes bite at dawn and dusk" caption. Nothing else makes 3D blocks look solid as cheaply as shadows.
3. **Weather you can see.** Rain particles scaled to the area station's last reading, with the station pinned in 3D and a dated caption. A2 asks for weather in the forecast; today weather is a number in a popup.
4. **A time-lapse that reaches today.** Feature-state heights (no `setData` per frame), steps proportional to real time, a date ticker, a national-total sparkline, a pause on the 2020 peak, and a speed control. Then the story can show 2019 → 2026 in 15 s without cheating.
5. **The chat drives the 3D.** Any answer about an area flies the island to it and pulses the prism; answer cards get "Walk it in 3D"; 3D states get URLs (`#plan/jurong-west/3d/stop/3`, `#map/3d/history/2020-08-21`) so the presenter can jump anywhere and judges can open a link on their phone.

**Rigour claims a sharp judge could challenge.**
- The "two-week" lead is overstated: forecasts are issued the day after the label week ends, but CDA publishes about a week later, so the live forecast issued 10 Oct for the week of 11 Oct had a real lead of 1–7 days. Add an issue-lag parameter and report lead time from publication (`pipeline.py:113`, `area_forecast.py:132`, README:164).
- The `ai_forecast` benchmark (81.8 vs 14.4) is fitted on raw counts from 2019 with the outbreak years in; Databricks judges may read it as unfair to their own function. Report a fair variant too (log counts, 104-week window).
- The area outlook's persistence baseline is scored as a hard 0/1 prediction, which caps its AUC and inflates its Brier score; leave-one-year-out trains on future years; no confidence intervals although the 1,100 examples come from 20 origin dates; "12 of 44 new High areas flagged" has no false-alarm count. Use a transition-matrix persistence, forward chaining, bootstrap intervals by origin date, and a reliability diagram.
- The champion uses weather but the no-weather challenger is marginally better on 2023+ (28.5 vs 28.7), the model loses to the baseline in 2025 (15.1 vs 13.2), and Diebold–Mariano p is 0.26. All disclosed; the champion/challenger framing invites the question. A gated promotion (challenger must beat champion) answers it.
- The ledger's source of truth is a JSON file with seed rows; Delta is a mirror (`ledger.py:22-57`).
